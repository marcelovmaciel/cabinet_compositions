L138: cite71†Jair Bolsonaro 's tenure as the 38th cite72†president of Brazil began with his inauguration on 1 January 2019, and ended on 1 January 2023.^{cite73†[2] } Bolsonaro took office following his victory in the cite68†2018 general election , defeating cite74†Fernando Haddad .^{cite75†[3] } His presidency ended after one term in office, following his defeat in the cite76†2022 general election to cite77†Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva .^{cite78†[4] } In the years Brazil has been a democracy since cite79†1985 , Bolsonaro became the first president to lose an election as an incumbent.^{cite78†[4] }
L139: His government was characterized by the strong presence of ministers with a military background, international alignment with the cite80†populist right and autocratic leaders,^{cite81†[5] }^{cite82†[6] } and was recognized for his anti-environmental,^{cite83†[7] }^{cite84†[8] } cite85†anti-indigenous people ^{cite86†[9] }^{cite87†[10] } and cite88†pro gun policies.^{cite89†[11] }^{cite90†[12] } He was also responsible for a broad dismantling of cultural,^{cite91†[13] }^{cite92†[14] } scientific and educational government programmes,^{cite93†[15] }^{cite94†[16] }^{cite95†[17] } in addition to promoting repeated attacks on democratic institutions^{cite96†[18] }^{cite97†[19] } and spreading cite98†fake news .^{cite99†[20] } His government was responsible for a significant reduction in bureaucracy and modernization of public systems, with the fast-paced digitization of federal public services, through the creation of the digital platform "gov.br".^{cite100†[21] } Bolsonaro also sanctioned the Economic Freedom Act, reducing bureaucracy in economic activities and facilitating the opening and operation of businesses throughout the country, which proved very beneficial, especially for small companies.^{cite101†[22] }
L140: During his administration cite102†crime dropped across the country^{cite103†[23] } and cite104†unemployment rates slowly fell,^{cite105†[24] } with the Brazilian cite106†GDP showing a moderate growth rate, averaging 1.5% per year.^{cite107†[25] } At the same time, cite108†job insecurity ,^{cite109†[a] } cite110†inflation ^{cite111†[30] } and cite112†hunger increased,^{cite113†[31] } while cite114†per capita income , cite115†social inequality and cite116†poverty reached its worst levels since 2012.^{cite117†[32] }

L148: Bolsonaro, at the time of his election, was a 27-year member of cite121†Congress ; his victory has been credited to voter anger at the political class over years of cite122†corruption in politics, cite120†economic recession , and a surge in cite102†violent crime .^{cite123†[33] } According to sociologist Clara Araújo, "the dissatisfaction over the economic crisis, it seems to me, was channeled along with a discourse about conservative morals".^{cite124†[34] } The economy of Brazil was recovering from crisis, with an unemployment rate of 12 percent at the time of the election, double that of five years prior.

L174: ### Economy
L175: 
L176: [cite168†edit ]
L177: Bolsonaro spent the first months of his administration trying to pass legislation and enact reforms with the stated goal of creating more economic freedom and growth for Brazil.
L178: The federal government cut taxes for some specific sectors of the economy and removed import tariffs for several areas, such as capital goods, and IT or telecommunication products which were necessary for technological evolution and increased production in the medical, industrial, and agricultural sectors.^{cite169†[52] }^{cite170†[53] }^{cite171†[54] } Bolsonaro also removed the import tariffs of cite172†sailing ships , cite173†jet skis and other luxury items.^{cite174†[55] } The government also passed a controversial cite175†pension reform , along with making other changes across the economy.^{cite176†[56] } The government also tried to pass economic reforms such as the Plano Mais Brasil, which enveloped around resource decentralization combined with greater fiscal responsibility in Brazil's management, reduction of taxes for productive sectors of the economy, reduction of direct state investments in the economy, and the debureaucratization of the state, while increasing funding for anti-poverty programmes.^{cite177†[57] } Some of those proposals were able to pass in Congress, though others were stopped in different committees.
L179: Overall, though the economy did grow, poverty and inequality expanded under Bolsonaro.^{cite178†[58] }
L180: According to the economist Guilherme Delgado, the proposed measures made by Bolsonaro disorganized the public service, stifled investments, privileged the rich, weakened democratic rights and deepened economic and social inequalities.^{cite179†[59] }
L181: The result of Bolsonaro's economic reforms were mixed. Overall, prior to the cite180†COVID-19 recession , the cite181†Brazilian economy was recovering and growing, though at a slower pace than predicted by the government.
