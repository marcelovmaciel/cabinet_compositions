# Cabinet Composition in Brazil, 2015-2026

## How to read this history

This history follows formal personal party affiliation among qualifying cabinet officeholders from 1 January 2015 through 19 March 2026. It explains how the represented party set changes across Dilma Rousseff's second administration, Michel Temer's administration, Jair Bolsonaro's administration and Lula's third administration. The accompanying [structured audit](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md) supplies the complete event chronology and evidence catalog. The account concerns the project's frozen historical coding; it does not equate cabinet membership with a party's political support for the government.

Officeholding and affiliation are separate. A minister can change party while continuing in the same office, and an office can change hands without changing the represented parties. Cabinet composition is the union of the parties of all qualifying officeholders. A departing minister removes a party only when no other qualifying member remains. The president and vice-president do not contribute by those roles, although a separate qualifying ministerial office can contribute. Ordinary substitutions during a continuing titular's leave do not create new cabinet members; documented replacements in genuinely vacant offices do.

Dates describe the state at the close of each day. Intervals include their starting date and exclude their ending date: service ending on 31 March contributes through 30 March. Effective dates, actual assumption of duties and publication of formal acts take precedence according to the existing coding rules; announcements, signatures and later ceremonies can have different dates. The bounded affiliation changes discussed below use the latest admissible date as their primary boundary. Service uncertainties retain their specifically recorded baselines, including two composition-neutral successions whose baseline is not the latest candidate. A selected boundary is not necessarily an exact historical observation. [Coding and interval conventions](../CODEBOOK.md).

The personal states `PARTY`, `UNAFFILIATED` and `UNKNOWN` remain distinct. An explicit historical affiliation observation can support subsequent continuity under the project's rule. Sponsorship of an appointment, party employment, military service and absence from a searched list do not by themselves establish personal affiliation or its absence. Some uncertainties have finite alternatives that make no difference to the cabinet union because another minister independently represents the potentially relevant party. That circumstance establishes the aggregate result without settling the individual's affiliation.

Other affiliations remain unrestricted `UNKNOWN`. For those intervals, the primary analysis uses a separate documented assumption that the unresolved officeholder contributes no additional party. These are `PROVISIONAL_MODEL_ASSUMPTION` records, not observations of non-affiliation. Twelve assumptions cover 100 distinct calendar days, with some overlapping. A historical period marked `primary_provisional` may contain many otherwise adjudicated days because unchanged sets are combined into one period; only the exact assumed dates are provisional. [Primary assumptions](../data/normalized/primary_assumptions.csv); [Decision V5ROOTD002](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5rootd002).

Party names follow the historical display conventions. PMDB and MDB, PR and PL, and PRB and Republicanos retain stable identities across renames. A merger can instead replace predecessor identities in the represented set. The history below uses these historical identities and labels; the published election-calendar files subsequently translate complete sets to election-year identities under the separate [contract](../CONTRACT.md).

## Dilma Rousseff's second administration

The [opening cabinet](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-opening-dilma_2) represented ten parties: PCdoB, PDT, PMDB, PP, PR, PRB, PROS, PSD, PT and PTB. Its first contraction followed Cid Gomes's departure from Education. PROS disappears on the coded publication-effective boundary of 19 March 2015, leaving nine parties. The departure is distinguished from the preceding day's events by the closing-state convention. Luiz Cláudio Costa's replacement service contributes PT, already present elsewhere; his affiliation rests on a dated personal description in an earlier appointment profile and accepted continuity. The personnel succession therefore removes PROS without adding another party. [Evidence LEG-8702e7355c32](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-leg-8702e7355c32), [NEW-COSTA](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-new-costa); [Decision R01](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r01); [19 March transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2015-03-19).

Considerable institutional change could occur inside that stable nine-party set. The October 2015 reorganization and ministerial appointments are coded from 5 October, when publication and possession establish the operative boundary, rather than the 2 October announcement. Offices were transformed and personnel moved, but the represented party union did not change. This is why an account based only on the number or names of ministries would produce a different chronology. [Evidence and Decision C024](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c024).

PROS returned through a personal switch. On 18 March 2016, the retained contemporary report places George Hilton's departure from PRB and accession to PROS on the same day. He continued at Sport, so the cabinet exchanged PRB for PROS without a change of minister. His subsequent departure is separately coded at the 31 March succession, when Ricardo Leyser entered. The 23 March announcement is not used as the end of qualifying service. PROS then disappeared again, while Leyser's PCdoB affiliation added no new party. [Evidence AFF-001](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-001); [Decision R02](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r02); [18 March](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2016-03-18) and [31 March transitions](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2016-03-31).

Two further departures narrowed the set before the administration boundary. Gilberto Occhi's exit from National Integration removes PP on 14 April. The coding distinguishes his 13 April resignation request from the publication-effective boundary; the decision also preserves the limitation that the detailed act was not independently retrieved. Gilberto Kassab's departure from Cities removes PSD on 19 April, using formal dismissal rather than his earlier request. Their successors did not add an otherwise absent party. Other contemporary turnover, including Maria Fernanda Ramos Coelho's brief service and Patrus Ananias's return, left PT represented. From 19 April through 11 May the coded set was PCdoB, PDT, PMDB, PR, PT and PTB. [Decisions R03](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r03), [R04](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r04), [C005](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c005); [14 April](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2016-04-14) and [19 April transitions](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2016-04-19).

## Michel Temer's administration

The administration boundary on 12 May 2016 replaced that six-party set with eleven represented parties: DEM, PMDB, PP, PPS, PR, PRB, PSB, PSD, PSDB, PTB and PV. The aggregate change reflects the incoming roster as a whole. Subsequent departures by individual ministers often left one of these parties represented elsewhere, while same-party replacements preserved membership in an office. The audit separates these personnel events from the much smaller number of changes to the union. [Opening Temer roster, decisions and evidence](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2016-05-12).

The first recurrent changes concerned Fernando Bezerra Coelho Filho at Mines and Energy. His formal parliamentary interruptions removed the cabinet's PSB witness for one day on 10 and 25 October 2016, 26 April 2017 and 2 August 2017; his return the following day restored PSB each time. Paulo Pedrosa occupied the genuine vacancies. Pedrosa's personal state remains `UNKNOWN`, bounded by the accepted alternatives PSDB and unaffiliated. Independent PSDB ministers cover those alternatives, so his uncertainty does not supply PSB or change the aggregate result. The 2 August primary set has a separate provisional dependency arising from Ivani dos Santos's service. These short gaps are actual episodes in the coded chronology, not a continuous absence stretching between their first and last occurrence. [Evidence NEW-COELHO-SERVICE](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-new-coelho-service); [Decisions C041](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c041), [C067](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c067); [Ivani's evidence C035](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c035).

PSB's final disappearance occurred on 18 October 2017, at another interruption in Coelho Filho's service. The party's own account establishes acceptance of his membership departure on 25 October. He returned to the ministry on 26 October coded as unaffiliated, so this return did not restore PSB. The distinction between the two dates matters: the cabinet lost its PSB officeholder before he ceased to be a PSB member. His accession to MDB on 21 March 2018 also left the union unchanged because that party was already represented. The current dated record supersedes the older broad departure window preserved in R10. [Evidence and Decisions C006](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c006), [C007](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c007); [18 October transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2017-10-18).

The next contraction came through Raul Jungmann's personal affiliation. His departure from PPS on 21 March 2018, supported by a dated letter reproduced in the retained report, removed PPS while he remained in qualifying service. On 2 April, Maurício Quintella Lessa's departure from Transport, Ports and Civil Aviation removed PR. His successor Valter Casimiro Silveira is coded unaffiliated from entry: the accession report explicitly distinguishes his personal non-affiliation from PR sponsorship. That observation governs the current coding despite contrary shorthand labels and an older unresolved description. Meanwhile, Ricardo Barros's replacement by Gilberto Occhi preserved PP. [Evidence AFF-004](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-004), [C001](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c001); [Decisions R11](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r11), [C001](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c001); [2 April transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2018-04-02).

The larger round of departures on 6 April removed DEM through Mendonça Filho's exit from Education, but did not remove every party whose ministers left office. Sarney Filho's succession by Edson Duarte preserved PV. Henrique Meirelles had switched from PSD to MDB on 3 April; other witnesses already represented both parties, so neither his switch nor his later departure changed their presence. PRB likewise continued through the January succession from Marcos Pereira to Marcos Jorge de Lima. Its 3–5 January date alternatives, and the 6–7 April alternatives for the PV succession, concern the identity of the officeholder on particular days rather than the represented party. [6 April transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2018-04-06); [Evidence AFF-003](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-003); [Decisions R07](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r07), [R08](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r08), [R09](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r09).

The 15 May change from PMDB to MDB is a dated label change within the same organization, following the TSE decision. The final membership contraction came on 5 July, when Helton Yomura left Labour and Eliseu Padilha formally accumulated the portfolio. PTB then disappeared. MDB, PP, PRB, PSD, PSDB and PV remained through 31 December. This long final period includes a provisional final day because Carlos Henrique Menezes Sobral's affiliation during his 2018 service remains `UNKNOWN`; his later personal description is not projected backward. [Evidence AFF-045](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-045), [C012](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c012); [5 July transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2018-07-05); [Decision V5W2D001](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w2d001).

## Jair Bolsonaro's administration

Bolsonaro's opening cabinet represented DEM, MDB, NOVO, PP and PSL. These are personal memberships: Damares Alves supplies PP, Ricardo Salles supplies NOVO, Osmar Terra supplies MDB, and Marcos Pontes supplies PSL alongside the DEM ministers. Bolsonaro's own party history does not determine the set. In particular, PSL remained represented after his political departure because Pontes remained affiliated. Nor does an organizational succession automatically enroll a former member: Augusto Heleno's coded departure from PRP precedes cabinet entry, so PRP's later incorporation does not contribute a successor party through him. [Opening roster](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2019-01-01); [Decisions R14](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r14), [R20](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r20).

The five-party set persisted until Rogério Marinho entered Regional Development on 11 February 2020, bringing PSDB. MDB disappeared a week later. Osmar Terra's earlier exit did not immediately remove it because designated replacement Welington Coimbra, known as Lelo Coimbra, continued MDB representation until 18 February. A similar vacancy mechanism brought MDB back for 16 April alone, when João Gabbardo dos Reis replaced Luiz Henrique Mandetta at Health before Nelson Teich's entry on 17 April. Gabbardo's current MDB coding rests on an explicitly dated historical PMDB observation and continuity; the earlier occupancy record's unresolved affiliation wording is not its final status. [Evidence C054](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c054), [C066](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c066); [11 February](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2020-02-11), [18 February](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2020-02-18), [16 April](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2020-04-16) and [17 April transitions](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2020-04-17).

Salles's continued tenure produced a different kind of change. The accepted interpretation of NOVO's statute retains membership during suspension of affiliate rights, despite reports using the language of suspended affiliation. It ends membership on the notified expulsion date, 7 May 2020. NOVO therefore leaves the cabinet months after the suspension, while Salles remains minister. The decision relies on the governing rule, notification and subsequent accounts of completed expulsion; it does not claim verification of a cancellation-register timestamp. [Evidence BOL-E03](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-bol-e03), [AFF-021](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-021), [AFF-023](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-023); [Decision R15](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r15).

The mid-2020 chronology combines an appointment with two bounded personal departures. Fábio Faria's entry at Communications brings PSD on 17 June. Marinho's PSDB departure is admissible on 16–18 June, with 18 June selected for the primary series. Contemporary positive observations, including a 15 June report, and a 19 June account of an already completed departure establish the current bounds. Later accounts placing the departure in February or extending PSDB membership into 2021 are retained but not selected. Thus the primary chronology shows PSD and PSDB together on 17 June; the accepted earlier exit alternatives need not. June 18 is an adjudicated boundary within a narrow window, not an exact registry date. [Evidence AFF-026](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-026); [Decision V5W6D03 and its supporting and conflicting evidence](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w6d03).

Damares's departure from PP is bounded between 4 and 19 September 2020. The latest positive observation is a 3 September report; a 20 September report describes a completed earlier departure. The primary series selects 19 September and thereafter codes her unaffiliated until her later Republicanos accession. Contemporary 2020 evidence outweighs preserved retrospective descriptions of a 2021 departure. This removes PP and leaves DEM, PSD and PSL. The absence of a precise withdrawal date changes the possible duration of PP representation, not the accepted subsequent unaffiliated interval beyond the latest bound. [Decision V5W6D02](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w6d02); [19 September transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2020-09-19).

Appointments then expanded the set. Gilson Machado Neto brought PSC to Tourism on 9 December 2020; Marcelo Álvaro Antônio's departure did not remove PSL because Pontes remained. João Roma brought Republicanos to Citizenship on 12 February 2021. Flávia Arruda brought PL to the Government Secretariat on 31 March, and Ciro Nogueira brought PP to the Casa Civil on 28 July. For Arruda and Nogueira, the evidence supports actual entry before a later public ceremony. By late July the union contained DEM, PL, PP, PSC, PSD, PSL and Republicanos. Marinho's own PL accession on 30 November added another PL member without expanding that union. [Decisions R24](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r24), [R23](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r23), [R22](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r22); [Evidence C010](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c010); [Decision V5W6D03](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w6d03).

The merger of DEM and PSL changes the organizational basis of representation on 8 February 2022. The TSE decision gives immediate effect to União Brasil, replacing the predecessor identities in the union. PL, PP, PSC, PSD and Republicanos remain alongside União. This is an organizational transition among continuing officeholders, rather than a new cabinet appointment. Tereza Cristina subsequently moves from União to PP within the bounded interval 1–4 March, with 4 March selected. Pontes independently represents União and Nogueira represents PP throughout that window, so every accepted date gives the same cabinet set. [Evidence AFF-044](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-044); [Decision BOL-A01](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-bol-a01); [8 February transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2022-02-08).

Onyx Lorenzoni's PL membership begins on the coded date 10 March while he remains Labour minister through 30 March. Contemporary accounts describe completed affiliation and an endorsed membership form, separately from the later 22 March celebration. The accepted date differs from later parliamentary status updates and is not presented as a recovered TSE processing timestamp. This switch does not bring PL into the cabinet: PL is already represented. Pontes also continues to sustain União through 26 March, so Onyx's switch does not immediately remove that party. [Evidence ONYX-E01](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-onyx-e01), [ONYX-E02](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-onyx-e02), [ONYX-E03](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-onyx-e03); [Decision ONYX-2022-01](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-onyx-2022-01).

The aggregate consequences become visible in the concentrated changes at the end of March. Faria's switch to PP removes PSD on 24 March. Pontes's and Roma's PL accessions remove União and Republicanos on 27 March, leaving PL, PP and PSC. Damares's and Tarcísio de Freitas's Republicanos affiliations restore that party on 28 March; Gilson Machado's PL signing removes PSC on 30 March. The published departures and successions of 31 March then remove the remaining PL contribution and restore PSD through Marcos Montes at Agriculture, while Cristiane Britto's ministerial succession preserves Republicanos. The resulting set is PP, PSD and Republicanos. União is therefore already absent on 27–30 March, before the departures of 31 March, and remains absent until a new witness enters in May. [Evidence AFF-026](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-026), [BOL-E21](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-bol-e21), [BOL-E22](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-bol-e22); [Decisions R14](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r14), [R18](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r18), [R23](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r23), [R24](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r24), [R25](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r25); [31 March transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2022-03-31).

Adolfo Sachsida's entry at Mines and Energy restores União on 11 May. Earlier historical compilations disagree about his affiliation, but the current decision accepts the official historical membership record linked to the officeholder by an exact identifier and predecessor continuity through the merger. It supersedes the earlier unaffiliated alternative while retaining those conflicting assertions in the evidence catalog. The four-party set then persists until 30 December, when Nogueira's departure removes the last established PP contribution and Helder Melillo Lopes Cunha Silva's vacancy service adds NOVO in the primary coding. Helder's registry record contains a departure-request date alongside regular status and a pending marker; the actual court-notice date remains unverified. NOVO and unaffiliated therefore remain credible alternatives for his two days of service. The primary year-end set is NOVO, PSD, Republicanos and União, additionally marked provisional for separate unresolved officeholders. [Decision V5W6D01](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w6d01); [Decision V5W5D001](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w5d001); [30 December transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2022-12-30).

## Lula's third administration

Lula's opening cabinet represented MDB, PCdoB, PDT, PSB, PSD, PSOL, PT, Rede and União. Personal affiliation explains features that a roster organized by appointment sponsorship might obscure. Waldez Góes contributes PDT: his leave from internal party activities did not establish departure from the party, and the retained account expressly separates him from União membership. On 13 September 2023, André Fufuca's entry at Sport brought PP and Sílvio Costa Filho's entry at Ports and Airports brought Republicanos. Márcio França moved from Ports to the new Entrepreneurship ministry while remaining PSB, so that transfer did not remove PSB. The resulting eleven-party set supplies the principal composition through most of the remaining period. [Opening roster](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2023-01-01); [Evidence AFF-038](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-038); [Decision R28](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-r28); [13 September transition](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2023-09-13).

The later recurrent changes mostly arise at Tourism. Celso Sabino's July 2023 accession did not introduce União, already represented elsewhere. His subsequent parliamentary returns created genuine ministerial vacancies that the service coding treats separately from his affiliation. Two were occupied by Carlos Henrique Menezes Sobral, whose qualified contemporary MDB description supports the primary affiliation while preserving an unaffiliated alternative. Other MDB ministers make that alternative composition-neutral. Five other vacancy spells were occupied by Ana Carla Machado Lopes, whose coded PSDB affiliation adds a party otherwise absent from the cabinet. [Decisions C073](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c073), [C074](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c074), [C075](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c075).

Ana Carla's five spells do not represent five new party accessions. One continuous affiliation supports them. The underlying observation is a historical Filiaweb copy dated 27 July 2016, recording PSDB entry on 2 October 2013. The decision links the rare full name to the officeholder using contextual identity evidence and applies forward continuity. Her later party employment corroborates identity rather than proving membership. A statement that she was not affiliated to União does not establish general non-affiliation. The evidence is an older historical observation with acknowledged limitations, not a current personal certificate. [Evidence and Decision C075](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c075).

Ana Carla's first qualifying spell brings PSDB into the primary set on 4 December 2023 and ends at Sabino's selected return on 6 December. That return remains bounded from 4 through 6 December. A retrospective Folhapress report says he reassumed on 4 December, while the retained parliamentary record documents his vote on 5 December and subsequent leave. The decision retains the conflicting actual histories rather than converting either an announcement or legislative administrative date into a definitive handoff. PSDB's two-day primary appearance can therefore shorten or disappear under an earlier return alternative. União remains represented independently throughout this episode. [Evidence C073](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c073), [V5W7E004](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-v5w7e004); [Decision V5W7D001](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w7d001).

PSDB returns from 3 through 5 December 2024, again through Ana Carla's vacancy service, before Sabino's 6 December return. Her occupancy here is a statutory-deputy adjudication based on her continuing executive-secretary appointment and the applicable decree; the repository does not claim a retrieved vacancy-specific nomination. Sabino's February 2025 interruption instead brings Sobral back for 1–2 February and produces no aggregate change. In April, Juscelino Filho leaves Communications, followed by successors coded unaffiliated. That removes another individual União witness, but Sabino still represents the party. Tourism consequently becomes decisive for União's later presence. [Decision C073](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c073); [Evidence C003](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-c003), [LEG-b5c3c93f0a0d](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-leg-b5c3c93f0a0d), [LEG-01028140cce5](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-leg-01028140cce5).

On 8 October 2025, Ana Carla's next vacancy spell replaces União with PSDB in the represented set; Sabino's return on 9 October reverses the exchange. His party's suspension of activities and ethics proceedings are coded separately from cancellation of affiliation, so they do not themselves remove União during his continuing service. Another departure on 13 November again exchanges União for PSDB. The primary return on 17 November restores União, but the actual handoff remains bounded from 14 through 17 November. [Evidence AFF-034](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-aff-034); [Decision C073](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-c073); [8 October](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2025-10-08) and [13 November transitions](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#event-transition-2025-11-13).

The November sources establish different observations within that window. Ana Carla exercised the acting role at a 14 November ceremony, which does not exclude a later handoff that day. Official reporting establishes Sabino's ministerial activity on the morning of 17 November. The Chamber's notification at 19:36 that evening dates parliamentary leave, not ministerial possession. Those records support the retained bounds without establishing which of 14–17 November is the exact closing-state transition. Earlier admissible returns shorten PSDB's primary four-day appearance and restore União correspondingly sooner. [Evidence V5W7E001](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-v5w7e001), [V5W7E002](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-v5w7e002), [V5W7E003](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#evidence-v5w7e003); [Decision V5W7D002](CABINET_HISTORY_AUDIT.md#decision-v5w7d002).

The final União exit occurs through affiliation rather than officeholding. The retained 8 December 2025 report describes the party executive's cancellation decision and Sabino's acknowledgment of expulsion. He remains in service coded unaffiliated until his final departure on 19 December. Ana Carla then contributes PSDB for 19–22 December; Gustavo Feliciano's actual entry on 23 December ends that contribution, with his personal state coded unaffiliated. The current service table and C073 use 19 December for Sabino's exit, despite the older R27 rationale's 23 December wording; the audit flags that discrepancy. From 23 December through the 19 March 2026 cutoff, the represented set is MDB, PCdoB, PDT, PP, PSB, PSD, PSOL, PT, Rede and Republicanos. The January Justice succession has bounded service dates but no party-set consequence because both adjoining officeholders are explicitly unaffiliated. ## Remaining uncertainties

The most consequential finite date alternatives concern Marinho's PSDB exit on 16–18 June 2020, Damares's PP exit on 4–19 September 2020, and Sabino's returns on 4–6 December 2023 and 14–17 November 2025. Their selected primary boundaries are the latest dates in their respective windows. Moving an event also moves its adjoining states; the alternatives do not permit arbitrary switching throughout the surrounding tenure. Tereza Cristina's 1–4 March 2022 window is masked by independent União and PP witnesses. The recorded January 2018 PRB and April 2018 PV succession baselines are exceptions to the codebook's general latest-date wording, retained as coded and composition-neutral. The January 2026 Justice uncertainty is also neutral. [Affiliation constraints](../data/normalized/sensitivity_constraints.csv); [Service constraints](../data/normalized/service_sensitivity_constraints.csv).

Finite personal alternatives remain for cases such as Helder Melillo, whose late-2022 NOVO contribution can disappear, and for individuals whose alternatives are masked by other ministers. Paulo Pedrosa and Marcos Galvão remain personally `UNKNOWN` between the accepted PSDB and unaffiliated possibilities. Maria Fernanda Ramos Coelho, Alberto Alves and Sobral's later service have qualified primary affiliations or non-affiliation with retained alternatives.
